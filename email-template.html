PROFESSIONAL ETIQUETTE · CLEAR COMMUNICATION

Communicate with Purpose.

Professional email communication creates a first impression before a conversation even begins. A clear subject line, respectful tone and well-organised message can help communicate interest and credibility.

01 / THE PURPOSE

Writing Emails That Work

Email remains an important communication tool in academic and professional environments. Students may use it to contact lecturers, request information, apply for opportunities or communicate with potential employers.

A professional email should make its purpose clear, provide the necessary context and communicate respectfully. It should be concise enough to read easily while still including the information the recipient needs to understand the request.

The following example demonstrates how I could contact an organisation to enquire about an industrial attachment opportunity in Data Analytics.

03 / ANATOMY OF A PROFESSIONAL EMAIL

Why Each Part Matters

01

Subject line

Summarise the purpose of the email in a few specific words. Avoid vague subjects such as "Hello" or "Important".

02

Professional greeting

Use the recipient's name or appropriate professional title when known. Otherwise, "Dear Hiring Manager" is a suitable option for this example.

03

Clear purpose

Introduce yourself and explain why you are writing near the beginning. This helps the recipient understand your request quickly.

04

Relevant information

Include details that support your request, such as your programme of study, the opportunity you are seeking and your relevant foundational skills.

05

Polite closing

Thank the recipient and state what response or information you would appreciate. Remain courteous without sounding demanding.

06

Signature and attachments

Provide your name and contact details. If you mention an attached CV or application letter, make sure the files are actually attached before sending.

04 / BEFORE YOU CLICK SEND

The Professional Email Checklist

A few simple checks can prevent avoidable mistakes and make your message more effective.

01

Check the recipient

Confirm that the email address belongs to the intended person or organisation.

02

Proofread your message

Correct spelling, grammar, punctuation and formatting errors.

03

Keep the tone professional

Use respectful language, complete sentences and appropriate greetings.

04

Verify your attachments

Open your CV and application letter before attaching them, and check that the filenames are professional.

05

Respect the recipient's time

Keep the message focused and allow a reasonable period for a response before sending a polite follow-up.

05 / COMMON MISTAKES

Small Details, Lasting Impressions

AVOID

Vague subject lines

A subject such as "Hi" does not explain what the message concerns.

Better: Data Analytics Industrial Attachment Enquiry
AVOID

Overly casual language

Slang, unexplained abbreviations and excessive punctuation may make a formal request appear careless.

Better: Use courteous, clear and complete sentences.
AVOID

Unverified claims

Do not exaggerate technical skills or claim experience, qualifications or projects that you have not completed.

Better: Describe your current skills honestly and explain what you want to learn.
