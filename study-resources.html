01 / MY LEARNING APPROACH

Study to Understand, Not Just to Remember

Studying Business Intelligence and Data Analytics involves more than memorising definitions. It requires understanding concepts, applying techniques to practical problems and interpreting the results. A topic may seem clear when reading notes, but genuine understanding becomes more apparent when I can explain the concept or use it independently.

My approach is to combine focused study sessions with practical exercises, revision and reflection. For technical subjects such as Excel and SQL, regular hands-on practice is especially valuable. Writing queries, correcting errors and working through examples can help transform theoretical knowledge into usable skills.

I also recognise the importance of asking questions and seeking support when I encounter difficulties. Challenges are opportunities to identify gaps in my understanding and decide what I need to practise next.

02 / EFFECTIVE STUDY STRATEGIES

Make Every Study Session Count

These strategies can help organise learning, improve understanding and support long-term retention.

01

Active Recall

Close your notes and try to explain a concept from memory. Answer practice questions before checking the solution.

Try this: Explain the difference between a database table, a row and a column without looking at your notes.
02

Spaced Revision

Review material across several days instead of trying to learn everything in one long session immediately before a test.

Try this: Review today's SQL lesson tomorrow, again later in the week, and once more the following week.
03

Learn by Doing

Apply ideas through practical exercises. Small, repeatable tasks help reveal what you understand and where you need more practice.

Try this: Write an SQL query, run it, inspect the output and correct any errors.
04

Break Down Complex Topics

Divide a large topic into smaller learning objectives. Understand the foundations before moving to more advanced techniques.

Try this: Learn SELECT and WHERE before progressing to more complex joins and subqueries.
05

Teach What You Learn

Explaining a concept to someone else can reveal gaps in your understanding and help you organise your thoughts.

Try this: Explain how a spreadsheet formula works using a simple example.
06

Reflect and Improve

After studying, identify what went well, what was difficult and what you should focus on during your next session.

Try this: Write down one concept you understand and one question you still need to answer.

04 / CONSISTENCY OVER INTENSITY

A Practical Weekly Study Routine

This is a sample routine that can be adjusted around lectures, assignments, personal commitments and assessment dates.

MONDAY

Review and Organise

Review lecture notes, identify difficult concepts and set three realistic learning priorities for the week.

30–45 minutes
TUESDAY

Practise Excel

Work through spreadsheet exercises and practise formulas, filtering, calculations or charts.

45–60 minutes
WEDNESDAY

Practise SQL

Write and test database queries. Review mistakes and record the solutions to problems you found challenging.

45–60 minutes
THURSDAY

Explore Something New

Spend time exploring a beginner lesson in programming, AI or data visualisation.

30–45 minutes
FRIDAY

Consolidate Learning

Test yourself on the week's topics and revisit concepts that still feel uncertain.

30–45 minutes
WEEKEND

Build and Reflect

Complete a small practical exercise, make progress on an assignment and reflect on the week's learning.

Flexible

The routine is a guide, not a strict timetable. Consistent, focused practice is more useful than following a schedule that is unrealistic for you.
